Preparation shortens the first conversation and lifts the quality of the reply. A Brisbane brief also differs from a generic national campaign: local SEO for map results, technical work for page-speed and indexation, eCommerce work for category and product pages, and content built around keyword-mapped pages each lead somewhere different. Before you enquire, line up:

  • Your service area: the suburbs and regions you actually serve, since a local angle comes from explaining the service area, not from dropping suburb names into copy.
  • A short keyword wish list: the terms you believe buyers use, so the intent behind each page can be tested.
  • Access details: analytics and search-console style read access where held, plus who approves website changes.
  • A budget band: knowing whether you sit closer to a $800 starting audit or an ongoing retainer shapes scope early.
  • Your decision deadline: whether you need movement this quarter or are building for the next year.

What a written audit should actually cover

The published entry point is a free written audit rather than an immediate sales call. A worthwhile version separates technical blocks from content gaps and local visibility issues. It reviews how easily Google can read the site, whether pages are indexed, Core Web Vitals, on-page improvements, internal links, other sites linking in, and the top three competitor gaps for the main keyword. Each category then points to different work:

  • Technical fixes: crawl, indexation and structured data checks before new content is commissioned.
  • Content mapping: keyword-mapped pages and topical authority built without pages competing against each other.
  • Local visibility: Google Business Profile, directory consistency, service pages and review strategy for map results.
  • Competitor gaps: what the top three rivals rank for that your site does not yet address.

A proposal that only describes traffic goals is harder to compare than one listing the actual work, the expected timeline and the monthly reporting method.

Budget bands and honest timelines

Concrete scoping signals help set expectations before any retainer begins. Retainers in Brisbane typically run from $1,200 to $4,000 plus GST per month, depending on competition, scope and the number of target keywords. Treat these as scoping signals rather than a promise every site fits the same band: a small local service business may need practical small business SEO, while competitive law, finance or national retail work needs a longer technical and content runway.

Timing follows competition. Most Brisbane businesses see measurable ranking movement in 3 to 6 months for mid-competition keywords. Tougher law, finance and national online retail terms can take 9 to 12 months, so plan scope against your actual competition level rather than a headline average.

Clear the myths before you commit

Two misconceptions cause most bad engagements. The first is the first-page guarantee. Google controls the algorithm, so no provider can promise positions, and credible work instead points to a transparent method, realistic timelines and monthly reporting. The second is the idea that locality means stuffing suburbs into copy. A Brisbane brief earns its local angle when it explains the service area, the buyer intent behind each page, and how map visibility differs from organic rankings. A trades business chasing map pack results has a different problem from an online store converting demand across Queensland, and the brief should say which one you are. Questions to send with your enquiry

Attach these to your first message so the reply addresses your situation rather than a generic package:

  • Does the audit prioritise technical fixes, content mapping and local search work separately?
  • Which target keyword set and intent sit behind each proposed page?
  • If link earning is included, does it use digital PR, supplier mentions or resource placements?
  • How will monthly reporting tie to completed work, not just traffic graphs?
  • Is Google Ads proposed as short-term capture while organic momentum builds?
  • Can the audit, timeline and price be stated in writing before payment?

The strongest comparison is between providers willing to put scope, evidence and timing in writing first, giving you something concrete to review alongside the service mix rather than broad claims about visibility.

  1. Prepare your brief. Gather your service area, keyword wish list, access details, budget band and decision deadline before contacting anyone.
  2. Request the written audit. Send the site details and service area so the first response can focus on the current website, not a generic package.
  3. Read findings by category. Separate technical blocks, content gaps, local visibility issues and competitor gaps before judging any proposal.
  4. Compare quotes in writing. Shortlist providers willing to state the audit, timeline and price in writing before payment, then review scope against the service mix.
